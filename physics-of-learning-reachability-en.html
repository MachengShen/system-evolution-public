1. Why Learning Must Be Irreversible Computation That Generates Heat

In classical mechanics (Liouville's theorem) or isolated quantum mechanics (unitary evolution $U^\dagger U = I$), microscopic dynamics are reversible: phase-space volume is conserved, von Neumann entropy remains invariant ($\Delta S = 0$), and zero heat is generated ($\Delta Q = 0$).

Yet purely reversible computation can never accomplish learning. There are three fundamental reasons:

  1. Learning Is a Many-to-One Contractive Projection of State Space:
    Prior to learning, a system's hypotheses or policy distributions over the environment are dispersed across a large phase-space volume $\Omega_{\text{prior}}$ (high prior uncertainty $H(S_{\text{prior}})$). After interacting with the environment and encountering prediction errors, the system must erase falsified branches and collapse redundant degrees of freedom, converging its internal structure onto a far more compact posterior manifold $\Omega_{\text{post}} < \Omega_{\text{prior}}$.
  2. The Landauer–Sagawa–Ueda Thermodynamic Cost:
    By the generalized Landauer principle and the second law of information thermodynamics, when a system acquires mutual information $\Delta I$ from environmental observations and compresses the entropy of its internal hypothesis space by $\Delta S_{\text{compress}} > 0$, the erased degrees of freedom of falsified trajectories cannot vanish into thin air—they must be exhausted into the surrounding thermal bath as irreversible heat:
    Law I · Generalized Landauer Heat Bound for Learning $$\Delta Q_{\text{learn}} \ge k_B T \ln 2 \cdot \Delta H_{\text{erased}}$$
  3. Forgetting Costs More Energy Than Remembering (Still–Sivak–Bell–Crooks Predictive Thermodynamics Identity):
    Still et al. (PRL 2012, Thermodynamics of Prediction) proved that for any physical system operating in a non-stationary environment, the unrecovered work dissipated at each step is strictly proportional to its "Nostalgia"—redundant historical information that remembers the past but provides zero predictive power regarding the future:
    Identity I-b · Still–Sivak–Bell–Crooks Nostalgia Dissipation Law $$\beta W_{\text{diss}} = I(S_t ; X_{\le t}) - I(S_t ; X_{>t}) = I_{\text{nostalgia}}$$
    Corollary (Structural Metabolism & Sleep Pruning): A genuinely efficient learning machine must continuously undergo irreversible "structural metabolism" (self-shedding / synaptic sleep pruning), distilling rote memorization of the past ($I(S_t; X_{\le t})$) into a minimal sufficient statistic for the future ($I(S_t; X_{>t})$). The heat generated by learning is, in essence, the entropic slag expelled when forging high-dimensional raw historical trajectories into low-dimensional predictive structures for the future.

2. 'Minimum Energy, Fastest Reach of a Future': The Thermodynamic Speed Limit (TSL)

The second pillar of our core definition—"reaching a target future fastest at minimum energy expenditure"—hits the exact bullseye of one of the most important breakthroughs in modern non-equilibrium statistical physics: the Thermodynamic Speed Limit (TSL) and Optimal Transport Geometry (Wasserstein Geometry).

Let the system's current state distribution be $p_0$ and the target future state distribution be $p_{\text{future}}$. Driving the system to that future within a finite time horizon $\tau$ requires a minimum irreversible entropy production (energy dissipation $\Sigma = \Delta Q / T$) strictly lower-bounded by the Benamou–Brenier / Aurell–Ito–Van Vu Thermodynamic Speed Limit inequality:

Law II · Thermodynamic Speed Limit (TSL) $$\tau \cdot \Sigma \ge \frac{\mathcal{W}_2^2\big(p_0, p_{\text{future}}; g(E)\big)}{2 D}$$

where:

  • $\tau$ is the time required to reach the target future (Time-to-Future);
  • $\Sigma$ is the irreversible energy/heat dissipated during the transition (Energy Dissipation, $\Sigma = \Delta Q / T$);
  • $\mathcal{W}_2\big(p_0, p_{\text{future}}; g(E)\big)$ is the order-2 Wasserstein geodesic distance from the current state to the target future under the system's current cognitive-substrate metric tensor $g(E)$;
  • $D$ is the kinetic mobility of the system.

This physical inequality directly defines both "Intelligence" and "Learning":

1. Executing Intelligence = Approaching the Thermodynamic Geodesic ($\tau \cdot \Sigma \to \min$) Given a fixed substrate, an unintelligent system either wanders in prolonged blind search (huge $\tau$) or brute-forces compute while burning massive heat (huge $\Sigma$) to stumble upon the target future. A highly intelligent system evolves along the least-action Wasserstein geodesic of the information manifold, reaching the future with the minimal action-dissipation product $\tau \cdot \Sigma$.
2. Cross-Rollout Learning (Metric Sculpting) = Contracting the Geometric Distance $\mathcal{W}_2$ If physics locks down $\tau \cdot \Sigma \ge \mathcal{W}_2^2 / 2D$, how can a system become both faster ($\tau \downarrow$) and more energy-efficient ($\Sigma \downarrow$) in the next episode? The only way is to use the irreversible computation of the current episode to permanently sculpt its own metric tensor $g(E)$ (e.g., forming synaptic priors, compiling skill caches, or carving $O(1)$ symlink shortcuts in the external substrate $E$).
Metric Sculpting Across Rollouts $$\mathcal{W}_2\big(p_0, p_{\text{future}}; g(E_{t+1})\big) \ll \mathcal{W}_2\big(p_0, p_{\text{future}}; g(E_t)\big)$$

This is completely isomorphic to our empirical findings in Trajectory Rollouts & Substrate Reinforcement Learning (Substrate RL): across 505 production sessions, the $8.89\%$ detour exploration dissipation (Detour Heat) serves precisely as the annealing energy required to locate high-impedance bottlenecks and compress $O(k)$ blind search into $O(1)$ lookup shortcuts.

It is not that an isolated person or machine is 'smart' in a vacuum, because every intelligent system is Grounded. In other words, intelligence is the capacity to continually position oneself across different regions—or construct new ones—where each region sustains a very low-entropy, well-preserved information structure."

This insight inverts traditional "individual computationalism" (the brain-/weight-centric view) into a Grounded Atlas of Low-Entropy Viability Basins, resolving from first principles of physics the thirty-year Stability–Plasticity Dilemma (Catastrophic Forgetting) in neural networks and cognitive science:

4.1 Why Talking About Individual Intelligence Apart from a "Grounded Region" Inevitably Causes Catastrophic Forgetting

In conventional machine learning, practitioners attempt to cram all knowledge of the world into a single globally shared parameter matrix $\theta$. When the system moves from environment $A$ into environment $B$, the new task gradient $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_B$ directly overwrites the principal Hessian eigen-directions of old tasks, collapsing prior information structures (Catastrophic Forgetting).

From statistical physics and Grounded Cognition, this happens because no low-entropy information structure can sustain itself in isolation from an external coupled substrate:

  • The low conditional entropy $H(S \mid E_\alpha) \ll H_{\max}$ of any finite agent is never maintained by brute-forcing internal weights in a vacuum; it is anchored by a Lyapunov potential basin $V_\alpha(x)$ jointly spanned by the coupled agent–environment–tool region $\mathcal{R}_\alpha = (S, E_\alpha)$.
  • As long as the system resides within this Grounded Region $\mathcal{R}_\alpha$, the geometric constraints of the environment and external memory substrates (file systems, bodily morphology, ecological niches) automatically exert an orthogonal restoring force on internal representations, keeping the local information structure inside a thermodynamically safe basin immune to catastrophic washout.
